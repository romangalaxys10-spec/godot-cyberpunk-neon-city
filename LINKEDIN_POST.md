I let GLM 5.3 build a full 3D open-world driving game.
Not a demo. A real, drivable, 3.6 km cyberpunk city.

Here's what it did — almost all of it on its own:

- A 3,500+ meter procedural city: 1,500+ instanced buildings, 49 districts, crosswalks, billboards, animated pedestrians, live traffic on both axes.
- A 4-wheel driving model: progressive steering, drift, weight transfer, damage, boost/NOS, heat & police pursuit.
- Day/night lighting, rain + lightning, airliners with strobes crossing the skyline.
- A working WebAssembly export you can drive in your browser. No install.

The honest part: one model did the building. GLM 5.3 wrote the whole engine, the physics, the AI, and even the first-pass textures. Gemini 3.7 Flash had a single job — making those textures look better. It wrote zero gameplay code.

That split is the whole point. A strong reasoning model can carry a project end-to-end. A vision model is a specialist you hire for the finish.
